History of Present Illness
IDSA 2003 Summary: A young adult with a swollen left knee.
A young adult man was admitted with a three-year history of swelling and induration of the left knee. Multiple nodules on the knee revealed draining sinuses.
Physical Examination
The temperature was 36.5ºC (97.7ºF). Swelling of the left knee extended to the distal third of the thigh. There were numerous woody hard nodules and sinus tracts with drainage (Figure 1). There was no regional lymphadenopathy.
Figure 1. Left knee.
Studies
The level of hemoglobin was 14.8 g/dL, white blood cell count (WBC) 9,000/mm³ (leukocytes 70.5%). The erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) was 82 mm per hour. A radiograph of the left knee (Figure 2) showed soft tissue swelling and lytic lesions at the medial cortex of the femur, with cortical thinning and osteoporosis.
Figure 2. Radiograph of knee.
Clinical Course Prior to Diagnosis
Computed tomography (CT) of the leg showed exostosis and multiple small lytic lesions with ill-defined margins in the internal femoral condyle. (See Figure 3.)
Figure 3. CT scan.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Biopsies from deep tissue and bone revealed the presence of sulfur granules (Figures 4 and 5). A gram stain of the specimens demonstrated the presence of beaded, branched, gram-positive filamentous rods, consistent with <i>Actinomyces</i>. Cultures were negative.
Figure 4. Stain of biopsy.
Figure 5. Stain of biopsy.
Treatment and Followup
Based on the histology, the patient was diagnosed with actinomycosis. Incision and drainage of the abscesses was performed and penicillin (300,000 UI/Kg/day) was administered for six weeks. Subsequently, he received a 6-month course of oral amoxicillin. Treatment images are shown in Figures 6 and 7.
Figure 6. Radiograph of left leg.
Figure 7. Follow-up.
Discussion
Final Diagnosis
Actinomycosis of the left knee, with bone involvement.
