History of Present Illness
IDSA 2001 Case Summary: A 9-day-old infant with fever and hypotension.
A 9-day-old infant had an anatomic defect at birth that required abdominal surgery. She then developed fever and hypotension. She had been receiving total parenteral nutrition (TPN) through a central venous line.
Physical Examination
The temperature was 39°C (102.2°F), blood pressure 104/52 mm Hg, and pulse 148 per minute.
Studies
The white blood cell count (WBC) was 25,000/mm³. Results of laboratory tests of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) parameters were normal, and the CSF culture was sterile. Blood cultures were positive; colonies that grew on blood agar after 48 hours are shown in Figure 1.
Figure 1. Culture results.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Pseudomonas oryzihabitans (formerly called Flavobacterium oryzihabitans) was identified. This organism is notable for its unusual yellow, rough colonies that are oxidase-negative.
Treatment and Followup
Discussion
Pseudomonas oryzihabitans has recently gained recognition as a cause of sepsis in immunodeficient patients with central venous access. This organism has an environmental reservoir similar to other non-fermentative gram negative rods.
Final Diagnosis
Line-related sepsis due to Pseudomonas oryzihabitans (formerly called Flavobacterium oryzihabitans) in an infant.
