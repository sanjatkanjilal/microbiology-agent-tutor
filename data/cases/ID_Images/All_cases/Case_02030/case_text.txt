History of Present Illness
Summary: A woman, status post bone marrow transplant, with a non-productive cough.
A woman in her forties underwent an allogeneic bone marrow transplant for acute myelocytic leukemia (AML) eight months prior to the onset of her symptoms. Her bone marrow transplant was complicated by graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) requiring tacrolimus (FK506), rapamycin, methotrexate and steroids. In addition, she was enrolled in a trial and received either daclizumab or placebo. She began to note fevers to 101.4°F (38.6°C), and a non-productive cough two days before admission.
Past Medical History
She had a history of acute myelocytic leukemia (AML), status post autologous bone marrow transplant, and graft versus host disease (GVHD).
Medications
Medications included prednisone (10 mg per day), rapamycin (3 per day), tacrolimus (2.5 daily), dapsone (100 mg daily), and acyclovir (400 three times daily).
Family History
Her immediate family was healthy.
Epidemiological History
There was no history of travel, sick contacts or pets, and no tobacco or alcohol use.
Physical Examination
The patient appeared ill and in moderate respiratory distress. The temperature was 100.2°F (37.9°C), heart rate 124 beats/minute, blood pressure 100/60 mm Hg, and oxygen saturation 90% on room air. The chest was clear to auscultation.
Studies
The white blood cell count (WBC) was 6,100/mm³, hematocrit 31%, and platelets 96,000/mm³.
A chest radiograph showed bibasilar opacities (Figure 1). Computed tomography (CT) of the chest delineated bibasilar infiltrates with nodular features (Figure 2).

Figure 2. Chest CT scan.
Clinical Course Prior to Diagnosis
She was admitted to the hospital; vancomycin and levofloxacin were initiated empirically for pneumonia.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Cytomegalovirus (CMV) viral load by hybrid capture was positive at 6.6 pg/ml. Bronchoscopy with trans-bronchial biopsy was sent for culture and pathology. Pathological examination of the specimens showed was chronic inflammation, consistent with diffuse alveolar damage and cytomegalovirus inclusions. In addition, immunohistochemical stains confirmed the presence of CMV (Figure 3).
Figure 3. Immunohistochemical stain of biopsy.
Treatment and Followup
Discussion
Immunocompromised patients, particularly transplant recipients, are at risk for CMV pneumonitis. Median time to presentation after transplant is approximately 45 days. Patients often present with dry cough and dyspnea.
Radiographic studies may be unremarkable, although ground glass opacification, infiltrates or nodules can be observed. Diagnosis often requires bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) with transbronchial biopsy to confirm CMV culture and histologic evidence of infection.
Final Diagnosis
Cytomegalovirus (CMV) pneumonitis in a patient after bone marrow transplantation.
