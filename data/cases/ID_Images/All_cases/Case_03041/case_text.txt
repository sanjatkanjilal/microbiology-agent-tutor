History of Present Illness
Summary: An African woman in her twenties with a rash.
An African woman in her twenties reported a 3-month history of a rash.
Epidemiological History
The patient was heterosexual and lived in South Africa.
Physical Examination
The patient was well-appearing and afebrile. There were shotty cervical and inguinal lymph nodes. There was an annular plaque involving the muzzle area of the face, a plaque on one thigh and an ulcerating lesion on the other thigh (Figures 1 through 3). She also had pigmented lesions on the palms and soles (Figures 4 and 5). There were no mucosal lesions. The remainder of the examination was normal.

Studies
Results of routine laboratory testing and a chest radiograph were normal. Screening and confirmation testing for human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) was positive.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Resutls of a Wassermann reaction test for syphilis was positive with a titer greater than 1:32.
Treatment and Followup
Intramuscular benzathine penicillin 2.4 million units weekly was administered for 3 weeks.
Discussion
Secondary syphilis is important to consider when macular lesions on the palm and sole lesions are detected. In the setting of HIV infection many different syphilitic rashes may present simultaneously. Tinea typically presents as an annular rash with scale. The rash associated with acute HIV is typically a viral exanthem and does not have significant dermal infiltration. The skin lesions of leprosy and systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) are important to consider, however these processes are not usually associated with macular lesions on the palms and soles.
Final Diagnosis
Secondary syphilis in an African patient with HIV infection.
