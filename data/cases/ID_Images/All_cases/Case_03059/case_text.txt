History of Present Illness
Summary: A man with chest pain and dysphagia.
A man in his fifties presented to the emergency room with a history of chest pain and dysphagia.
Epidemiological History
The patient was from El Salvador, and had recently arrived in the United States. He had a history of smoking 20 cigarettes per day.
Studies
Results of a complete blood count (CBC), blood urea nitrogen, creatinine and liver function tests were normal, and a test for syphilis (RPR) was non-reactive.

Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
The chest CT scans and barium swallow revealed mega-esophagus. Serologic testing for Chagas disease was positive.
Treatment and Followup
Discussion
Chagas disease, caused by Trypanosoma cruzi, is present only in the Western hemisphere. Most cases occur in Central and South America. Chronic Chagas Disease may cause cardiomyopathy or gastrointestinal tract dysfunction (megadisease). In South America, both cardiomyopathy and megadisease are seen, whereas in Central America megadisease seems to be less common; the reason for this difference in presentation is not clear.
The most common clinical manifestation of chagasic gastrointestinal disease is mega-esophagus, which typically presents with dysphagia. Other common sites of chagasic gastrointestinal disease are the colon, the parotid glands, and the stomach.
Final Diagnosis
Chagas mega-esophagus, caused by Trypanosoma cruzi.
