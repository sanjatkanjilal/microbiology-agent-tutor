History of Present Illness
IDSA 2001 Case Summary: A woman from the Philippines with abdominal pain and fever.
A woman in her thirties from the Philippines reported a three-month history of episodic abdominal discomfort, fever and weight loss of 5 kilograms. Over the three days prior to admission, the patient developed worsening abdominal pain, vomiting and watery diarrhea.
Past Medical History
There was a history of jaundice at age 14 years and “pelvic infection” treated 6 years previously by a local physician.
Epidemiological History
The patient had grown up in a rural area in the Philippines.
Physical Examination
The temperature was 98°F (36.7°C). The patient was pale, with no jaundice. The abdomen was distended, with firm hepatosplenomegaly and ascites (Figure 1).
Figure 1. Physical finding.
Studies
The level of hemoglobin was 10.3 g/dL, albumin 29 g/L; alkaline phosphatase 797 U/L and gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT) 227 U/L (elevated). Computed tomography (CT) of the abdomen was performed (Figures 2 and 3).
Figure 2. Abdominal CT scan.
Figure 3. Abdominal CT scan.
Clinical Course Prior to Diagnosis
Endoscopic examination, performed to evaluate the thickened ileocecum seen in the CT scan, showed an ileocecal mass (Figure 4).
Figure 4. Endoscopy.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
An ileocecal biopsy of the mass showed caseous granulomas (Figure 5). A core biopsy of the liver showed granulomatous inflammation and a giant cell (Figure 6). Acid fast bacilli (AFB) found on the liver biopsy were consistent with abdominal tuberculosis.
Figure 5. Biopsy of ileocecal mass.
Figure 6. Core biopsy of the liver.
Treatment and Followup
The patient improved on standard 4-drug antituberculous treatment and subsequently returned to the Philippines to continue therapy.
Discussion
Final Diagnosis
Abdominal tuberculosis with an ileocecal mass and liver involvement.
