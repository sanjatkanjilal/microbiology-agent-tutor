History of Present Illness
IDSA 2001 Summary: A man in his fifties with a painful and red finger.
A man in his fifties presented to an Emergency Department with a red painful middle finger on the right hand.
Twelve hours before presentation, he cut down a tree with his left hand, while holding onto the tree with his right hand. He did not recall any trauma. Initial examination revealed a red, swollen finger. There was no drainage after a lateral incision. He was prescribed cephalexin and was discharged.
Twenty-one hours later, he returned to the emergency department with fever, chills, and increasing pain from the palm to the axilla.

Clinical Course Prior to Diagnosis
Penicillin, clindamycin and aztreonam were administered emergently.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
The patient was taken to the operating room for incision and debridement, and a diagnosis of necrotizing fasciitis was made. Wound culture grew Streptococcus Group A.
Treatment and Followup
The patient recovered completely.
Discussion
Final Diagnosis
Group A Streptococcal necrotizing fasciitis.
