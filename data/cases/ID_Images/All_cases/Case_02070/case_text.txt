History of Present Illness
Summary: A man with pneumonia.
A man in his sixties was admitted because of fevers and chills for four days, and cough (without sputum) for two days. He was disabled due to long-standing rheumatoid arthritis and was on prednisone (10 mg every other day).
Past Medical History
He had rheumatoid arthritis and was status post gastrectomy for peptic ulcer disease. There was no other significant past medical history or epidemiologic exposure.
Medications
He took prednisone, as above.
Physical Examination
His temperature was 105°F (40.6°C), and his lungs were clear.
Studies
The white blood cell count was 9,200 cells/mm³ with 79% polymorphonuclear leukocytes and 3% band forms.
The initial chest radiograph revealed a nodular infiltrate in the left upper lobe (Figure 1). Sputum gram stain revealed polymorphonuclear leukocytes with mixed oral flora. Bronchoscopy and cultures were unrevealing.
Figure 1. Chest radiograph.
Clinical Course Prior to Diagnosis
A chest radiograph five days later showed a worsening infiltrate (Figure 2).
Figure 2. Chest radiograph five days later.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Following empiric antibiotic treatment, a diagnosis of Legionella micdadei infection was made retrospectively by testing acute and convalescent sera for causes of atypical pneumonia.
Treatment and Followup
The patient improved on empiric therapy that included erythromycin.
Discussion
The commensal organisms that promote the growth of Legionella pneumophila do not support L. micdadei, perhaps accounting for the lower frequency of the latter in the water supply. Legionella micdadei usually affects individuals who are immunosuppressed or have undergone prolonged hospitalization. At the time of this publication, treatment of L. micdadei pneumonia should include a macrolide or a fluoroquinolone. In immunosuppressed individuals, a longer course of therapy (up to 21 days) may help prevent relapse.
Final Diagnosis
Legionella micdadei atypical pneumonia.
