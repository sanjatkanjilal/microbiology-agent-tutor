History of Present Illness
Summary: A woman presented with back pain and a hoarse voice.
A woman in her fifties was well until four months prior to presentation when she developed mid-scapular back pain that she described as dull and gnawing. Three weeks before presentation, she developed a hoarse voice. She also noted progressive weakness, a non-productive cough and shortness of breath. One week before, mild facial swelling developed. During this period, she lost 20 pounds.
Past Medical History
Negative.
Physical Examination
There was edema of the face and bilateral upper extremities. Heart sounds were distant and indistinct. A faint diastolic murmur was heard in the 2nd right intercostal space, without radiation. On direct visualization, her left vocal cord was completely paralyzed.
Studies
A chest radiograph showed a mass in the upper, anterior portion of the right chest, which displaced the heart, trachea and esophagus, and was consistent with an ascending aortic aneurysm (Figure 1).
Figure 1. Chest radiograph.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
A rapid plasma reagin (RPR) test was reactive at 1:8 and the fluorescent treponemal antibody absorbed (FTA-ABS) test was positive.
Treatment and Followup
The aneurysm was resected and pathology showed changes consistent with syphilitic aortitis.
Discussion
The lack of any constitutional symptoms argues strongly against subacute bacterial endocarditis, actinomycosis, and a mycotic aneurysm. Sclerosing mediastinitis due to histoplasmosis is a fibrosing process not an aneurysmal dilatation. This presentation is typical for syphilitic aortitis. The pathology of syphilitic aortitis is endarteritis obliterans involving the vaso vasorum of the aorta, resulting in aneurysmal dilatation. The ascending aorta is the most common site of involvement. Complications may include aortic regurgitation and coronary artery stenosis.
Final Diagnosis
Syphilitic aortitis caused by Treponema pallidum.
