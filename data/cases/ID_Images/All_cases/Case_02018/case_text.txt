History of Present Illness
IDSA 2002 Case Summary: A man with skin nodules.
A man in his twenties presented with a one-month history of a nodular exanthem that developed over his right forearm and head. The rash was not tender or pruritic, and had failed to improve after an empiric course of cephalexin. He reported no history of weight loss, fever, loss of appetite, cough, chest pain or diarrhea.
Epidemiological History
He had immigrated to the United States from an island in the Pacific two years before. There was no history of illicit drug use, or exposures to pets or other animals.
Physical Examination
He appeared well-nourished. The temperature was normal. There were erythematous maculopapular lesions on his forehead and malar areas, right tragus and pinna. A confluent, raised plaque-like area along the right forearm had decreased sensation to pinprick testing. There was a similar nodule on the dorsum of the right second metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joint, with synovial thickening along the extensor digitorum longus tendons. (See Figures 1, 2, and 3.) The remainder of the examination was normal.

Studies
Testing for antibodies to human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) was negative. Test results for the complete blood count (CBC) and serum chemistries were normal.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
A skin biopsy was performed, and pathologicial examination of the tissue with Kinyoun stain is shown (Figure 4), revealing organisms consistent with Mycobacterium leprae.
Figure 4. Kinyoun stain of skin biopsy.
Treatment and Followup
The patient was treated with dapsone, rifampin and clofazimine. At one-month follow-up, lesions were regressing in size and erythema, and no new neurologic lesions had developed.
Discussion
Mycobacterium leprae grows best at 27-33°C, and lesions of leprosy seem to have a predilection for cooler areas of the skin. Lepromatous leprosy is characterized by multibacillary disease, with erythematous macules, papules and/or nodules. Nerve damage results either from direct bacterial invasion or perineural granulomatous inflammation.
Final Diagnosis
Lepromatous leprosy.
