History of Present Illness
Summary: A woman with a headache and dizziness.
A middle-aged woman presented with headache and dizziness.
Past Medical History
She had hypertension and diabetes mellitus type II, and was status post partial hysterectomy.
Medications
Medications included conjugated estrogen, enalapril, and glyburide.
Family History
Her mother had a history of a benign brain tumor.
Epidemiological History
The patient had been raised in a rural area, and lived in a city in a mid-Atlantic state. She denied tobacco, alcohol or drug use. She had a dog, but denied exposure to farm animals. She had traveled to Texas and Mexico two decades earlier.
Physical Examination
Her temperature was 37°C (98.6°F), pulse 90 beats per minute and regular, blood pressure 150/95 mm/Hg, and respiratory rate 18 per minute. She had no lymphadenopathy. The lungs were clear. There were no cardiac murmurs. The abdomen was soft and there was mild right upper quadrant tenderness, but no enlargement of liver or spleen. On neurologic exam, she was alert and oriented and cranial nerves were intact. There was mild ataxia on tandem gait. The remainder of her examination was normal.
Studies
The results of a complete blood count (CBC), electrolytes and tests of renal function were normal. The level of glucose was 211 mg/dL, and aspartate aminotransferase (AST, SGOT) 39 U/L; other liver function tests were normal. A chest radiograph showed a normal heart size and no infiltrates.
Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the head showed multiple ring-enhancing lesions scattered throughout the white matter of both hemispheres (Figures 1 and 2).

Clinical Course Prior to Diagnosis
The patient underwent a brain biopsy of a right frontal lesion. Pathologic examination of the tissue is shown in Figures 3 and 4.

Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
The pathology of the brain biopsy showed trophozoites of Toxoplasma gondii (Figures 5 and 6). A test for antibodies to human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) was positive. The HIV RNA level was 510,000 copies/mL; CD4 T-lymphocyte cell count was 69 cells/mm³.

Figure 6. Tissue cyst of Toxoplasma.
Treatment and Followup
The patient received treatment with pyrimethamine and sulfadiazine, with improvement in her neurologic symptoms. She subsequently was also treated for HIV infection.
Discussion
This case illustrates the importance of considering underlying HIV disease in any patient that presents with focal brain lesions. If the patient had been diagnosed with HIV infection prior to the brain biopsy, then empiric therapy for toxoplasmosis would have been considered.
Toxoplasma encephalitis in patients with HIV generally presents with subacute onset of focal neurologic abnormalities. Imaging of the brain shows multiple ring-enhancing lesions, often involving the basal ganglia. In a patient with AIDS and focal brain lesions, alternative diagnoses must be sought if empiric therapy for toxoplasmosis does not result in clinical improvement within 10-14 days or if the patient clinically worsens.
Final Diagnosis
Toxoplasma gondii encephalitis in a patient with HIV infection and AIDS.
