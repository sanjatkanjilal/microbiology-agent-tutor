History of Present Illness
Summary: An African woman with fever, abdominal pain and eosinophilia.
A woman in her sixties from West Africa presented with fever, malaise, and abdominal pain. She noted poor oral intake for several weeks and had weight loss of several pounds (1-2 kilograms). She reported no history of nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea.
Past Medical History
None.
Medications
None.
Epidemiological History
She had immigrated to an urban area in New England many years before from West Africa. She lived in a tight knit community and participated in many traditional rituals. She did not drink alcohol.
Physical Examination
On examination, the temperature was 100.6ºF (38.1ºC) and there was tender hepatomegaly; the remainder of the examination was normal.
Studies
The white blood cell count was 9600 cells/mm³ with 35% eosinophils and the hematocrit was 25%. The level of alkaline phosphatase was 299 U/L; other tests of liver function were normal. Computed tomography (CT) of the abdomen revealed complex liver lesions (Figure 1).
Figure 1. Abdominal CT scan.
Clinical Course Prior to Diagnosis
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
A liver biopsy was performed. Pathological examination of the liver biopsy specimen (Figure 2) demonstrated an eosinophilic inflammatory infiltrate. A tunnel-like path, seen in Figure 2, was thought to represent a tract of one of the flukes. Serology returned positive for Fasciola at 1:256. Examination of a stool specimen for ova and parasites demonstrated a single Fasciola hepatica egg.
Figure 2. Pathological exam of liver biopsy.
Treatment and Followup
Triclobendazole was administered, with improvement
Discussion
Trichinosis, toxocariasis, and Fasciola can all cause significant eosinophilia. However, only Fasciola is associated with an eosinophilic hepatitis.
Final Diagnosis
Fasciola hepatica infection.
