History of Present Illness
IDSA 2001 Summary: A man with sinusitis.
A man in his twenties presented to a Otolaryngology clinic. He had a history of chronic sinusitis, including previous lengthy treatment courses with amoxicillin-clavulanate and levofloxacin, without resolution of symptoms.
Past Medical History
See above.
Epidemiological History
He worked in an office building and liked to garden, and had no significant travel exposures.
Physical Examination
The patient appeared well. There was purulent sinus drainage and the examination was otherwise normal.
Studies
An aspirate of the sinuses, obtained endoscopically, was sent for bacterial and fungal culture. Growth of the aspirate specimen, from the eighteenth day, is shown in Figure 1.
Figure 1. Culture growth.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
White, fluffy colonies grew on fungal media without cycloheximide within 2 days; clamp connections were seen, with no conidia. The “mushroom” structure developed after 18 days of incubation. Schizophyllum commune was identified. Figure 1. Culture growth.
Treatment and Followup
Discussion
The organism Schizophyllum commune is a basidiomycetous fungus that has been rarely reported to be a cause of sinusitis in both immunocompetent and immunocompromised hosts. The presentation of sinus disease due to Schizophyllum commune may clinically resemble Aspergillus sinusitis.
Final Diagnosis
Schizophyllum commune sinusitis.
