History of Present Illness
Summary: A young man with fever and rash.
A man in his twenties presented with a three day history of fever to 104°F (40° C), oral lesions, sore throat, and rash that began on his palms and soles and spread to his entire body.
Physical Examination
The temperature was 103°F (39.4° C). Numerous vesicular and erythematous oral lesions and exudative tonsillopharyngitis were present. There were numerous maculopapular lesions on the palms (Figure 1), soles, extremities, torso (Figure 2), back, and face.
Figure 1. Physical findings.
Figure 2. Rash on torso.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Testing for syphilis, antibodies to HIV and HIV nucleic acid were negative. A culture of the oral lesions grew Herpes Simplex Virus, type 1 (HSV-1). HSV-1 immunoglobulin G (IgG) serology was negative. Figure 1. Physical findings. Figure 2. Rash on torso.
Treatment and Followup
The patient improved with complete recovery of his oral and skin lesions after treatment with acyclovir.
Discussion
The acral distribution of the rash suggests Rocky Mountain Spotted Fever, syphilis, atypical measles or erythema multiforme. Acute HIV-1 may present with a maculopapular rash. The target-shaped rash of erythema multiforme may follow HSV-1 infection or mycoplasma infection, or may be due to a drug reaction.
Final Diagnosis
Erythema multiforme due to primary Herpes Simplex Virus, type 1 (HSV-1) infection.
