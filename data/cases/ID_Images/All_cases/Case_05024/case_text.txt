History of Present Illness
Summary: A young adult woman with abdominal discomfort.
A woman in her twenties presented with abdominal discomfort of 3-4 months' duration and weight loss of eight pounds.
Epidemiological History
The patient had emigrated from rural Cambodia to the United States several years earlier.
Physical Examination
The physical examination was normal.
Studies
The white blood cell count was 8,000/mm³ with 5% eosinophils. Other routine laboratory testing, including results of liver function tests, were normal. The level of CA-125 was elevated at 1445 U/ml.
Clinical Course Prior to Diagnosis
Computed tomography (CT) revealed paraaortic lymphadenopathy, omental and diffuse peritoneal thickening and a large pelvic mass (Figures 1 through 3).

Figure 3. Chest CT scan.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Laparotomy revealed diffuse peritoneal studding with nodules (Figures 4 and 5). Pathologic sections showed granulomata. Three weeks later, cultures grew Mycobacterium tuberculosis.
Figure 4. Laparotomy findings.
Figure 5. Laparotomy findings.
Treatment and Followup
Antituberculous therapy was administered and the patient improved clinically.
Discussion
A high index of suspicion, secondary to this patient’s epidemiological history and unusual age for pelvic neoplasm, was critical in this case. The diagnosis of peritoneal tuberculosis was considered despite the apparent pelvic mass (which was eventually determined to be loculated fluid related to inflammation), peritoneal studding and very high CA-125 test result.
Final Diagnosis
Tuberculous peritonitis caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis.
