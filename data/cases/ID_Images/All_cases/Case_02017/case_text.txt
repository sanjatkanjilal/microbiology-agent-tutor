History of Present Illness
IDSA 2002 Case Summary: A man with skin nodules on the right arm.
A man in his sixties presented with nodular skin lesions on his right upper arm.
In the spring, he had gone fishing in the Gulf of Mexico. His hook had inadvertently caught a rusty crab trap and, while attempting to free his hook, he had sustained a superficial, right dorsal hand laceration that never completely healed. Though it was not painful, it was mildly erythematous and spontaneously drained yellow purulent material. Over the following two months, four more lesions developed proximal to the initial lesion. He reported no history of fevers, chills or sweats.
Past Medical History
Non-contributory.
Medications
None.
Epidemiological History
He owned two dogs. His hobbies include fishing and gardening; he reported no rose bush, hay or sphagnum moss exposures.
Physical Examination
The temperature was 98.2°F (36.8°C), pulse 61 beats per minute, and blood pressure 125/76 mm Hg. There were 3 erythematous nodular lesions (1-3 cm in diameter) on the right upper arm and 2 smaller subcutaneous nodular lesions, with no erythema (Figures 1 and 2). The examination was otherwise normal, with no lymphangitic streaking or lymphadenopathy.

Studies
Results of laboratory tests were normal.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Biopsy of the forearm lesion revealed caseating granulomas; special stains and cultures were negative. An incision and drainage procedure of the dorsal hand lesion was performed. The acid-fast bacilli (AFB) stain was negative; however, the culture grew Mycobacterium marinum.
Treatment and Followup
Treatment with doxycycline was initiated. After 10 weeks of therapy, four of the five lesions had almost completely regressed. One lesion persisted, but was markedly decreased in size.
Discussion
Mycobacterium marinum may cause cutaneous infection after an exposure to contaminated waters. The organism grows poorly at temperatures greater than 37°C, which can contribute to the typical distribution of the skin lesions over upper and lower extremities.

Mycobacterium marinum skin infection.
