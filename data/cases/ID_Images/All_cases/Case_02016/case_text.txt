History of Present Illness
IDSA 2002 Case Summary: A man with scrotal pain.
A man in his fifties presented with scrotal pain of 12 weeks duration, which had failed to respond to several courses of oral antibiotics. He was hospitalized for refractory epididymitis and hyponatremia. Orchiectomy was performed after he did not improve with intravenous antibiotics. Empiric anti-tuberculous therapy was initiated pending pathology results.
On the third post-operative day, mental status changes developed that were felt to be attributable to pain medication and metabolic abnormalities. Two days later, he developed weakness on his right side. Family members reported he had had recent sweats but no fever.
Past Medical History
There was a history of diabetes mellitus and hypertension.
Medications
Medications included insulin, atenolol, and hydrocodone-acetaminophen.
Social History
He lived with his wife and did not smoke or drink alcohol.
Epidemiological History
He lived in Northern California. He had moved to the United States from India 20 years earlier, and had not traveled recently.
Physical Examination
The patient was thin. The temperature was 97.8°F (36.6°C). The neck was supple; the bowel sounds were hypoactive, and there was tenderness in all quadrants of the abdomen, extending into the inguinal regions. On neurologic examination, he was unintelligible and unable to follow commands. There was a right facial droop and right upper and lower extremity weakness.
Studies
The level of sodium was 126 mmol/L, glucose 220 mg/dL, white blood cell count (WBC) 8,900 cells/mm³. CD4 T-lymphocytes 224 cells/mm³ (38% of total lymphs). Testing for human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) was negative. Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) analysis revealed white cells of 353 cells/mm³ (89% lymphocytes), glucose 31 mg/dL, and protein 2,400. A stain for acid fast bacilli (AFB) was negative.
A chest radiograph showed no active disease or scarring. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of spine showed diffuse enhancement of the spinal cord between the T2-T6 thoracic verebrae, that was thought to be consistent with pachymeningitis, with no obstruction or focal defect. Treatment and Followup
The patient was treated with anti-tuberculous therapy and steroids, with initial improvement. The subsequent course was notable for lower extremity weakness and a thoracic epidural abscess, urinary retention and evidence of granulomas on prostate biopsy, and worsening mental status and neurologic signs when steroids were tapered.
Discussion
Final Diagnosis
Disseminated tuberculosis caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis including tuberculous epididymitis, prostatitis, pneumonitis, pachymeningitis and numerous tuberculomas of the brain in a patient with diabetes mellitus.
