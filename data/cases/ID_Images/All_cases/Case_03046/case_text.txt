History of Present Illness
Summary: A man with fever and chills.
A man in his seventies presented with a one-week history of fevers and chills. He denied any other systemic complaints. There was no history of nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, or rash.
Past Medical History
He had undergone a prostatectomy for localized prostate cancer.
Epidemiological History
He was sexually active with a single female partner. He denied any recent travel or any history of international travel, animal bites, or tick bites. He resided in a suburban community of northeastern Massachusetts in a home with a large garden. He also enjoyed golf.
Physical Examination
He was well appearing, with a temperature 102°F (38.9°C). The rest of the examination was normal, with no rash, lymphadenopathy, or hepatosplenomegaly.
Studies
The hematocrit was 35%, alanine aminotransferase (ALT) 70 IU/L, and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) 55 IU/L. Other routine laboratory testing was normal. Urinalysis was negative. A chest radiograph was normal.
Clinical Course Prior to Diagnosis
While in the hospital, the patient continued to have intermittent fevers. Cultures of the blood and urine were negative. A blood smear is shown (Figures 1 and 2).

Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
The blood smear revealed intraerythrocytic parasites consistent with Babesia microti.
Treatment and Followup
The patient was treated with azithromycin and atovaquone for 10 days with an excellent clinical response. Serologic testing for Ehrlichia and Lyme disease was negative.
Discussion
Given his geographic history, Babesia infection is much more likely than malaria or typhoid. The identification of the intra-erythrocytic maltese cross form is pathognomonic of Babesia infection. The development of fever without localizing symptoms in an endemic area for Babesia requires a careful examination for this organism. The absence of an identified tick bite is common.
Final Diagnosis
Babesia microti infection.
