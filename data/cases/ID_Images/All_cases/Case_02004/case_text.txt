History of Present Illness
Medical Student Curriculum Case Summary: A man in his thirties with acute myelogenous leukemia (AML) and a rash.
A man in his thirties with M4 acute myelogenous leukemia (AML) was receiving salvage chemotherapy with high-dose cytarabine (Ara-C) when he developed scattered pruritic erythematous papules across the trunk, axillae and upper extremities in the setting of profound neutropenia. Recent magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the abdomen had demonstrated multiple ring-enhancing lesions (less than one centimeter in diameter) in the liver and spleen.
Past Medical History
The past history included acute myelogenous leukemia (AML) and gastro-esophageal reflux disease (GERD).
Epidemiological History
The patient was originally from Peru. Family members, including young children, had visited recently.
Physical Examination
There were scattered erythematous papules across the trunk, axillae, and upper extremities (Figure 1).
Figure 1. Physical finding.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
A skin biopsy was performed. Pathological examination of the skin biopsy revealed a scabies mite within a parakeratotic scale (Figure 2).
Figure 2. Biopsy result with scabies mite.
Treatment and Followup
The patient was treated with 5% permethrin cream, with reapplication 1 week later. On further evaluation, the children who had recently visited were also found to have scabies infestation. The subcentimeter lesions in the liver and spleen were determined to be due to leukemic infiltration.
Discussion
Scabies is an ectoparasitic infestation by the mite, Sarcoptes scabiei. Transmission occurs by contact and nosocomial outbreaks are well described. At the time of this publication, treatment is typically topical with either lindane or permethrin.
Final Diagnosis
Scabies infestation, in a patient with acute myelogenous leukemia.
