History of Present Illness
IDSA 2001 Case Summary: A teenager with a sore throat, fever, and malaise.
A teenager presented with a five-day history of a sore throat, fever, nausea, cough and malaise. A rapid strep test was negative. Blood cultures were obtained and oral azithromycin was administered. The next day, her symptoms increased, and she was admitted with fever, emesis, dysphagia and shortness of breath.
Past Medical History
No significant past medical history.
Social History
She was an active student athlete, and had no history of recent travel.
Physical Examination
The patient appeared ill. The temperature was 38.4°C (101.1°F), pulse 110 beats per minute, blood pressure 116/68 mm Hg, and respiratory rate 40 breaths per minute, while breathing supplemental oxygen. There was tender anterior cervical lymphadenopathy with neck swelling bilaterally, greater on the left than the right. Examination of the chest revealed shallow respirations, guarding upon deep inspiration, and coarse rales bilaterally, with good aeration.
Studies
The white blood cell count (WBC) was 22,400 cells per cubic millimeter and platelets 820,000 per cubic millimeter. A chest radiograph and head scan were done and are shown in Figures 1 and 2.

Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Culture of the blood grew Fusobacterium. Ultrasonography of the neck (Figures 3 and 4) showed complete occlusion of the left internal jugular vein and partial occlusion of the right internal jugular vein.
Treatment and Followup
Lemierre's disease (postanginal septicemia) is the classical presentation of human necrobacillosis. The infection often begins in the throat with spread to the lateral pharyngeal space and internal jugular vein. The resulting septic internal jugular thrombophlebitis can be complicated by septic pulmonary emboli, as in this case. Although Fusobacterium necrophorum is the usual causative organism, polymicrobial bacteremia may occur.
Discussion
Final Diagnosis
LeMierre’s disease caused by Fusobacterium.
