History of Present Illness
Summary: An elderly man with fever, chills and abdominal pain.
A man in his eighties presented with four days of fever, shaking chills and crampy abdominal pain, without diarrhea.
Past Medical History
There was a history of hypertension and benign prostatic hypertrophy.
Physical Examination
The patient appeared ill; the temperature was 102°F (38.9°C). The abdomen was not tender and the pulses in the lower extremities were full.
Studies
The white blood cell count (WBC) was 11,000 cells/mm³. Computed tomography (CT) of the abdomen revealed extensive soft tissue stranding around the abdominal aorta with a focal aneurysmal dilatation below the right renal artery, consistent with a mycotic aneurysm (Figure 1). Blood cultures were positive.
Figure 1. Abdominal CT scan.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
The patient was taken emergently to the operating room, where a mycotic aneurysm of the infrarenal aorta was found. The area was debrided and the patient underwent right axillobifemoral bypass grafting. Operative specimens and blood cultures were positive for Salmonella enteritidis (Figure 2).
Figure 2. Gram stain from aortic aneurysm.
Treatment and Followup
The patient was treated with antibiotics, did well and was discharged home.
Discussion
A mycotic aneurysm is a localized dilatation of an artery due to infection in the vessel wall. Mycotic aneurysms arise following bacterial infection of a previously normal arterial wall or through secondary infection of a pre-existing aneurysm. In some series, Salmonella species are the second most common cause of mycotic aneurysms, after Staphylococcus aureus. Salmonella enteritidis is one of the non-typhoidal salmonellae and is commonly associated with foodborne outbreaks of diarrheal disease.
Final Diagnosis
Salmonella enteritidis aortitis.
