History of Present Illness
IDSA 2001 Case Summary: A man with weakness, weight loss and diarrhea.
A man in his sixties presented with a 4-week history of weakness, difficulty walking and diarrhea. He had lost 20 pounds over the previous 2-3 months. The patient reported no fever, chills, nausea or vomiting.
Past Medical History
He had a history of hypertension and had had multiple cerebral infarcts.
Epidemiological History
He had lived in Florida during the previous 6 months, and had no pets.
Physical Examination
The temperature was 38°C (100.4°F). On examination, there was hepatosplenomegaly; motor strength of the upper and lower extremities was decreased, which was not new.
Studies
The white blood cell count (WBC) was 3,000 cells per cubic millimeter, hematocrit 29%, and platelets 136,000/mm³. The level of serum urea nitrogen was 61 mg/dL and creatinine 3 mg/dL, and tests of liver function and stool studies were normal. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the abdomen showed bilaterally enlarged adrenal glands (Figure 1).
Figure 1. Abdominal MRI scan.
Clinical Course Prior to Diagnosis
An aspirate of the adrenal was performed; a special stain of the aspirate is shown in Figure 2.
Figure 2. Adrenal gland aspirate.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Gomori methenamine silver stain of the adrenal aspirate (seen in Figure 2) showed small budding yeast. Cultures of the bone marrow, blood and adrenal aspirate grew Histoplasma capsulatum.
Treatment and Followup
Discussion
Histoplasma capsulatum is endemic in the Ohio and Mississippi River valleys. Subacute progressive disseminated histoplasmosis may present with prolonged symptoms, such as weight loss, fever, and diarrhea.
Conidia are inhaled and convert to yeast inside pulmonary macrophages. Organisms invade the adrenal glands in approximately 80% of subacute progressive histoplasmosis. Overt Addison’s disease occurs in less than 10% of cases.
Final Diagnosis
Disseminated histoplasmosis caused by Histoplasma capsulatum.
