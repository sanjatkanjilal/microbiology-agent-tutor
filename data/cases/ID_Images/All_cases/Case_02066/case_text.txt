History of Present Illness
Summary: A woman with abdominal pain and a liver lesion.
A woman in her sixties developed anorexia and nausea of 4 weeks' duration, followed by 1 week of fever and right upper quadrant abdominal pain. She presented for evaluation, and denied any change in bowel habits.
Past Medical History
There was a history of diabetes mellitus and an endoscopic procedure with biopsy of a gastric ulcer had been performed 2 months before.
Medications
Medications included omeprazole.
Epidemiological History
She had traveled to India 6 months before the onset of her illness. She had also spent time on Cape Cod and worked in the healthcare industry.
Physical Examination
The temperature was 101°F (38.3°C). There was right upper quadrant abdominal tenderness, and the liver edge was palpable 1 centimeter below the right costal margin.
Studies
The white blood cell count (WBC) was 12,000 cells/mm³, total bilirubin 1.2 mg/dL, alkaline phosphatase 195 U/L, and alanine aminotransferase (ALT, SGPT) 50 U/L.
Clinical Course Prior to Diagnosis
Computed tomography (CT) of the abdomen (Figure 1) and aspiration of the liver lesion were performed. Gram stain of the liver aspirate is shown in Figure 2.
Figure 1. Abdominal CT scan.
Figure 2. Gram stain.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Direct fluorescent antibody stain and culture of the liver aspirate were positive for Actinomyces israelii. (See Figure 3.)
Figure 3. Fluorescent stain.
Treatment and Followup
The patient was treated with a prolonged course of penicillin and fully recovered.
Discussion
The most common sites of involvement with actinomycosis are the jaw, the lung and intra-abdominal disease. Hepatic actinomycosis represents about 5% of cases of actinomycosis. The infection may result either from direct extension from a contiguous focus or from hematogenous spread. It is usually related to a breach in the gastrointestinal mucosa, although extension from pleuro-pulmonary infection is sometimes seen. Other companion organisms may be present.
Final Diagnosis
Hepatic actinomycosis caused by Actinomyces israelii.
