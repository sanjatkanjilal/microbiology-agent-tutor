History of Present Illness
Summary: A middle-aged woman with leg ulcers.
A woman in her fifties developed a pruritic rash four months before admission. The rash became excoriated and developed into expanding non-healing ulcers with necrotic eschars.
Past Medical History
There was a history of alcoholism. There was no history of diabetes or other known immunosuppressive conditions.
Physical Examination
The patient appeared disheveled and chronically ill. The temperature was normal, and the othervital signs were stable. There were multiple deep 2-10 centimeter ulcers with eschars on both legs (Figures 1 and 2).

Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Surgical debridement was performed. Fungal wet prep revealed large non-septate hyphae with right angle branches (Figures 3 and 4). Cultures grew Rhizopus (Figure 5). Pathological examination confirmed invasive mucormycosis.

Treatment and Followup
This patient underwent multiple debridements, aggressive local wound care, and ambisome (10 mg/kg/day) resulting in slow improvement of her ulcers. Her caregivers suspected that this patient had an unrelated pruritic eruption four months before admission that became excoriated and then locally inoculated with Rhizopus. Her only clear immunosuppressive conditions were alcoholism and malnutrition.
Discussion
Treatment of cutaneous mucormycosis typically involves wide surgical debridement with aggressive antifungal therapy.
An outbreak of primary cutaneous mucormycosis occurred in the 1970s due to contaminated elastic bandages. Cutaneous mucormycosis may also occur after trauma or burns. Dissemination from the skin site can also occur.
Final Diagnosis
Cutaneous mucormycosis secondary to Rhizopus.
