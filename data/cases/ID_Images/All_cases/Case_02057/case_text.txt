History of Present Illness
Summary: A woman in her sixties with an infestation.
A woman in her sixties had a history of metastatic melanoma with innumerable in-transit metastases affecting her right leg. Several months prior to admission, and after receiving multiple cycles of experimental chemotherapy, she chose to initiate comfort care measures. She was bedbound at home, when she was admitted with a question of an infestation.
Past Medical History
As above.
Physical Examination
She appeared cachectic and ill, with multiple cutaneous nodules over her trunk, back, and legs. The largest in-transit metastases essentially covered her right lower extremity. The large nodules were necrotic and bleeding, and measured up to the size of grapefruits. The biggest necrotic melanoma nodule was on the inner aspect of her right thigh, with innumerable highly mobile forms (Figure 1).
Figure 1. Physical finding.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Speciation of the highly mobile, larval forms revealed common fruit fly larvae.
Treatment and Followup
Multiple methods of local control were attempted. The leg was covered with a thick layer of petroleum jelly twice daily, which resulted in rapid elimination of all live larvae.
Discussion
Myiasis refers to infestation by the larval forms of dipterous (two-winged) flies. Some species lay eggs directly on compromised tissue causing the entity of wound myiasis. Secondary bacterial infection may complicate wound myiasis. Although this problem occurs most often in the tropics, myiasis may occur in patients who have not left the United States.
Final Diagnosis
Myiasis in a necrotic melanoma nodule.
