History of Present Illness
Summary: A man with facial pain and diplopia.
A man in his fifties with a history of non-Hodgkins lymphoma presented with facial pain and diplopia of three days' duration.
Past Medical History
He had non-Hodgkins lymphoma, for which chemotherapy was administered two weeks before presentation.
Physical Examination
The temperature was 99.5°F (37.5°C). There was left sided proptosis (Figure 1) with pain and diplopia on upward gaze.
Figure 1. Physical finding.
Studies
The level of white blood cells (WBC) was 2,100 cells/mm³ and neutrophils 800 cells/mm³. Blood urea nitrogen, creatinine, and liver function tests were normal.
Computed tomography (CT) of the head and the sinuses was performed. (See Figure 2.)
Figure 2. Head CT scan.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
The CT scan shows complete opacification of the left maxillary sinus. There was erosion of the inferior orbital plate with the mass extending into the orbit below the inferior rectus muscle (arrow). (See Figure 3.)
A surgical debridement procedure was performed. Histologic examination of the resected specimen is shown. (See Figure 4.) Histopathology showed fungal elements, which were identified as Aspergillus fumigatus, with extension into bone.
Figure 3. Head CT scan.
Figure 4. Silver stain of debridement specimen.
Treatment and Followup
Following extensive surgical debridement, the patients was treated with systemic amphotericin B.
Discussion
Aspergillus may cause severe sinusitis, particularly in patients with neutropenia. Anaerobic infections generally develop in patients who have a history of chronic sinusitis. Given the broad differential diagnosis of the causes of sinusitis in a neutropenic host, debridement, histologic examination and appropriate microbiologic culture are key to management.
Final Diagnosis
Aspergillus fumigatus sinusitis in a patient with non-Hodgkins lymphoma.
