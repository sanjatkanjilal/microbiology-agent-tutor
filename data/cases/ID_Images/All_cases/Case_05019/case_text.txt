History of Present Illness
Summary: An elderly woman with finger swelling.
An elderly woman presented with pain, redness, and swelling of her left fifth finger that had gradually worsened during the next five days. There was no history of trauma or similar problems in the past. There were no other joint symptoms or fever.
Past Medical History
The patient had osteoarthritis and hypothyroidism, and had recently had an urinary tract infection.
Medications
Medications included furosemide, ciprofloxacin, acetaminophen, atenelol and levothyroxine.
Family History
There was a family history of gout.
Physical Examination
The patient appeared well, and younger than her stated age. The temperature was 100°F (37.8°C) and other vital signs were normal. There was redness and swelling of the entire left fifth finger, most marked on the dorsal surface of the distal phalanx. Fluctuance and maximal tenderness were present there as well. There was no lymphangitis or proximal lymphadenopathy. There was a decreased range of motion of both joints of the distal small finger, especially in the distal interphalangeal joint. There were chronic arthritic deformities of both hands. See Figures 1 and 2.

Studies
The white blood cell count was 13,810/mm³ (segmented cells 81.8%, lymphocytes 8.95, monocytes 7.4%, eosinophils 1.8%, basophils 0.1%), hemoglobin 14.0 g/dL, hematocrit 40.6%, platelet count 174,000/mm³ and the serum uric acid 9.4 mg/dL. Radiographs of the fifth finger showed degenerative joint changes consistent for age.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Aspiration of the fluctuant area on the dorsum of the distal interphalangeal joint yielded 0.1 milliliters of thick white material, which was almost entirely composed of needlelike crystals, consistent with gout. There were rare polymorphonuclear leukocytes present. Figure 1. Physical finding. Figure 2. Physical finding.
Treatment and Followup
Prednisone (40 milligrams daily for five days) was administered. Several days later, the symptoms were almost entirely resolved.
Discussion
Not all red, painful swelling of the distal finger is infectious. Non-infectious causes include acute inflammatory processes, such as crystal arthropathies. Aspiration of fluctuant areas can often yield data invaluable for appropriate treatment. The first clinical episode of gout may appear late in life.
Final Diagnosis
Crystal arthropathy, consistent with gout, of the left fifth finger.
