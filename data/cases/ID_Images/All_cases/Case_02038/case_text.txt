History of Present Illness
Summary: A man with fever, cough and dyspnea one year after a bone marrow transplant.
A man in his thirties developed weakness, fatigue, dry cough, and progressive exertional dyspnea of 2 days' duration, one year after an allogeneic bone marrow transplant for aplastic anemia. He presented for evaluation.
Past Medical History
He had had an allogeneic bone marrow transplant for aplastic anemia, which was complicated by skin and liver graft versus host disease (GVHD), and myasthenia gravis, and was status post thymectomy.
Medications
Medications included prednisone (30 mg per day), cyclosporine, mycophenolic acid, and dapsone.
Physical Examination
The temperature was 96.5°F (35.8°C). Oxygen saturation was 89% while breating room air. Skin examination revealed a desquamating rash consistent with his known GVHD. The lung examination revealed diffuse crackles.
Studies
The white blood cell count was 9,900 cells/mm³.
Clinical Course Prior to Diagnosis
Unfortunately, within 3 hours of presentation the patient expired. An autopsy was performed. Pathological examination of tissues obtained at autopsy are shown in Figures 1 and 2.
Figure 1. Mucicarmine stain.
Figure 2. Hematoxylin and eosin stain.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
The pathologic appearance of the tissues shown (Figure 2) is consistent with cryptococcosis.
Treatment and Followup
Discussion
The large capsule seen around each organism on histopathologic evaluation is diagnostic of Cryptococcus. Often, a mucicarmine stain is useful for demonstrating the organism. Cryptococcosis can cause disseminated disease months to years after transplantation.
Final Diagnosis
Disseminated cryptococcal infection in a patient status post bone marrow transplantation.
