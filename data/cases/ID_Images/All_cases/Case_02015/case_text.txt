History of Present Illness
IDSA 2002 Case Summary: A man with fever and headaches.
A man in his twenties presented with a three-day history of fever, memory loss, and severe headaches.
Past Medical History
He had diabetes mellitus, hypertension, chronic headaches for 1 year, and end-stage renal disease, and was on hemodialysis.
Medications
Medications included enalapril, atenolol, and calcium acetate.
Social History
There was no history of smoking, drug use, or alcohol.
Epidemiological History
He was from Mexico, worked in agriculture, and had traveled home during previous year. He reported no animal exposures.
Physical Examination
The patient appeared ill and tachypneic. The temperature was 38.1°C (100.6°F), blood pressure 225/113 mm Hg, pulse 134 beats per minute, and respirations 35 breaths per minute. He was alert, but oriented to self only. The site of a hemodialysis catheter located in the right subclavian vein appeared normal. There was nystagmus on right lateral gaze and the reflexes were hypoactive throughout.
Studies
The white blood cell count (WBC) was 15,800/mm³ and the serum level of sodium was 124 mmol/L, urea nitrogen 40 mg/dL, and creatinine 6.0 mg/dL. A chest radiograph was normal. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the head revealed lepto-meningeal enhancement of the midbrain (Figure 1).
Clinical Course Prior to Diagnosis
The patient was intubated for respiratory failure. He was treated empirically with four drug anti-tuberculosis therapy and intravenous amphotericin B and 5-fluorocytosine (5-FC). An external ventriculostomy drain was placed.
Analysis of ventricular cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) revealed 133 white cells per cubic millimeter (51% neutrophils, 26% lymphocytes, and 3% monocytes) and 291 red cells/mm³, with a protein level of 44 mg/dl and glucose level of 57 mg/dl. Analysis of the CSF for cryptococcal antigen, Histoplasma antigen, and herpes simplex virus (HSV) polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and an acid-fast bacilli (AFB) smear were negative, and bacterial cultures were sterile. Serum tests for cryptococcal antigen and human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) antibody were negative. A cerebral magnetic resonance (MR) study with angiography showed diffuse bilateral vasculitis and infarctions.

Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
The complement fixation tests for Coccidioides immitis antibody in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and serum were positive.
Treatment and Followup
He received intravenous itraconazole and intrathecal amphotericin B. Unfortunately, he remained comatose. In consultation with the family, medical support was withdrawn, and he expired.
Discussion
Meningitis is the most serious complication of Coccidioides infection, and typically involves basilar meninges. Vasculitic strokes are seen on computed tomography (CT) scanning in 40% of patients, and are associated with 70% mortality.
Final Diagnosis
Coccidioidal meningitis, caused by Coccidioides immitis, with vasculitic infarctions in a patient with diabetes mellitus and end-stage renal disease.
