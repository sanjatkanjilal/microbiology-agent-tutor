History of Present Illness
Summary: A woman with breast cancer and leukemia developed skin lesions.
A woman in her fifties, with a history of breast cancer and secondary M4 acute myelogenous leukemia (AML), developed multiple raised erythematous skin lesions during a hospitalization for chemotherapeutic treatment of AML. The chemotherapy had resulted in significant neutropenia prior to development of the skin lesions.
Past Medical History
There was a history of breast cancer, status post lumpectomy and axillary node dissection, and M4 acute myelogenous leukemia (AML).
Physical Examination
The patient appeared well, and vital signs were within normal limits. The site of a Hickman intravenous catheter appeared benign. Examination of the skin revealed a number of small palpable erythematous nodules with surrounding satellite lesions (Figure 1).
Figure 1. Physical finding.
Studies
The absolute neutrophil count was 220 cells/mm³. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the abdomen revealed multiple tiny enhancing nodules throughout the liver and spleen.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Culture of a punch biopsy of the skin grew Candida tropicalis.
Treatment and Followup
The patient was treated with liposomal amphotericin.
Discussion
Patients with hematologic malignancies and neutropenia are at special risk for disseminated Candida infection. Most Candida infections result from traversal of the mucosal barrier of the gastrointestinal tract or from intravenous catheters.
Hematogenous spread of Candida results in protean manifestations in virtually all organ systems, including such findings as microabscesses in the liver and spleen and skin lesions as described here. Cutaneous lesions due to disseminated candidiasis are generally nodules that are 0.5-1 cm in diameter and erythematous. The lesions may be single or scattered over the entire skin surface. Although these lesions may resemble those of ecthyma gangrenosum (which also afflicts neutropenic hosts), the presence of hepatosplenic lesions in this case suggested the diagnosis of disseminated candidiasis.
Final Diagnosis
Disseminated Candida tropicalis infection in a patient with acute myelogenous leukemia (AML) and neutropenia.
