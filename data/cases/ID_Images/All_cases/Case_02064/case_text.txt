History of Present Illness
Summary: A young man with fever and rash.
A previously healthy male in his twenties presented with 2 days of fever and rash. He denied any other symptoms.
Epidemiological History
The patient lived in New England and was sexually active with one female partner. He denied any recent travel or animal exposures.
Physical Examination
Oral and hand lesions were present (Figure 1).
Figure 1. Physical finding.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
The culture of one of the ulcers was positive for herpes simplex virus, type-1 (HSV-1).
A more detailed past medical history revealed that he had developed his first episode one year earlier, at which time he had had an oral ulcer.
Treatment and Followup
He was treated with acyclovir with complete resolution of his symptoms. Subsequently, he has had multiple further episodes of fever and rash.
Discussion
Erythema multiforme is most commonly due to HSV infection. The rash of erythema multiforme is typically target-shaped, as seen in this case. The rash associated with the other conditions noted in the differential diagnosis do not have this target-like pattern: syphilis typically has a papulosquamous eruption; acute HIV-1, a diffuse macular exanthem; RMSF, an acrally distributed petechiae; and erythema migrans, an expanding circular eruption.
Final Diagnosis
Erythema multiforme associated with herpes simplex virus infection.
