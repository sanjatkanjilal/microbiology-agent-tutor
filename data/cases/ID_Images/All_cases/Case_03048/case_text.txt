History of Present Illness
Summary: A man with right forearm and hand ulcerating lesions.
Three months earlier, a male in his thirties from the southern highlands of Peru had developed a firm nodule on his right hand that ulcerated. He recalled having an insect bite at the location of the lesion. Two weeks before, he developed a second lesion on his right forearm. He denied any fever, chills or sweats.
He had no response to topical or systemic antibiotics, and had used a salve of crushed plants with no improvement.
Past Medical History
None.
Medications
None.
Allergies
He had no known allergies.
Social History
He was married and had children.
Epidemiological History
He smoked one-half pack cigarettes per day, occasionally drank alcohol, worked in construction and occasionally harvested crops. He owned a dog, cats and chickens.
Physical Examination
His temperature was 37.2°C (99°F), pulse 68 beats per minute, respirations 14 per minute, blood pressure 120/75 mm Hg. He was a well-appearing, robust male. His examination was normal except for the skin of his right extremity. (See Figure 1.)
Figure 1. Right arm and hand lesions.
Studies
Complete blood count (CBC) and electrolytes were within normal limits. A Gram stain of the exudate was non-diagnostic. The exudative fluid was sent for bacterial culture, fungal culture and Giemsa staining.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Giemsa staining was negative for evidence of Leishmania and acid-fast and modified acid-fast stains were also negative. Bacterial and mycobacterium cultures were negative. Fungal culture grew filamentous colonies with a dark brown appearance. Microscopy of the colonies demonstrated hyphal forms consistent with Sporothrix schenckii.
Treatment and Followup
Sporotrichosis classically presents with an erythematous nodule that or ulcer, followed by development of multiple subcutaneous nodules that spread along the course of regional lymphatics. This pattern of nodular lymphangitis may also be seen with non-tuberculous mycobacterial infections (particularly Mycobacterium marinum), nocardiosis and cutaneous leishmaniasis. Rarely, botryomycosis, staphylococcal lymphangitis and tularemia present as a nodular lymphangitis. A skin biopsy and culture is critical in distinguishing among these different etiologies of nodular lymphangitis.
Discussion
Cutaneous sporotrichosis, with cultures consistent with Sporothrix schenckii.
